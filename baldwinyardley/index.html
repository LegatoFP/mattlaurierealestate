Orientation

One zip code, two very different lifestyles

Nearly all of this inventory carries a "Yardley, PA 19067" address and feeds the same well-regarded schools — but it splits into two distinct living experiences. Knowing which one a listing belongs to matters more than the address alone.

Yardley Borough

The walkable town · ~1 square mile

The historic riverfront core. A compact, leafy grid of older homes around Main Street, Lake Afton, and the Delaware Canal towpath — where you can walk to cafés, restaurants, the Saturday farmers market, and the train. Inventory here is genuinely scarce because the town is so small, so in-town homes tend to move fast and competitively.

Lower Makefield Township

The suburban surround · car-oriented

Wraps around the borough on three sides and holds the bulk of the homes in this price band — established 1960s–80s subdivisions plus newer planned communities, on bigger lots with a quieter, sparse-suburban feel. Highly educated, affluent, and family-driven, with parks, Five Mile Woods, and quick access to I-95 and Route 1.

Lifestyle

What it's like to live here

Beyond the numbers — the everyday amenities that make this stretch of Bucks County hold its value.

The Delaware Canal towpath

A flat, scenic 58-plus-mile trail along the canal runs right through town — Pennsylvania's 2022 Trail of the Year — used year-round for walking, running, biking, and fishing along the Delaware River.

Train to the city

The Yardley station on SEPTA's West Trenton Line gives a one-seat commute toward Philadelphia, with the borough core a short walk away. Center City is roughly 20–25 miles by car.

A real downtown

Main Street offers walkable shops, cafés, and riverside dining, a year-round producer-only Saturday farmers market, and free community events — outdoor movies, concerts, and the annual Harvest Day festival.

Parks & green space

Buttonwood, Lakeside, Memorial, and Edgewater parks in the borough; Veterans Park and the Five Mile Woods Nature Reserve in the township — plus Lake Afton's millpond setting and the river itself.

Pennsbury schools

The whole area feeds the highly-rated Pennsbury School District — a major, durable driver of demand and resale value across both the borough and the township.

A fast, competitive market

Homes in this band are moving in about a week at the median, with just over half selling above asking price — a sign of steady buyer demand against limited supply.
